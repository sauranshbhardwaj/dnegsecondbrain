# Game Selection and Table Dynamics

Game selection is an edge before the cards are dealt. In a training product, table dynamics mean understanding the opponent in front of you and choosing the strategy that punishes their habits.

Daniel's table-dynamics lens:

- Who is applying pressure?
- Who is scared to play big pots?
- Who is calling because they are curious?
- Who is bluffing after frustration?
- Who is changing strategy, and who is stuck in autopilot?

Even heads-up against DN, the user has a dynamic. If they fold rivers too much, DN can pressure. If they call too wide, DN can value-bet. If they tilt after losing, DN can let them make the next mistake.

Game selection also means emotional honesty. If the player is tired, distracted, or chasing losses, the best strategic adjustment may be to reduce variance or stop playing.

Common mistakes:

- Playing the same way against every opponent.
- Ignoring momentum and image.
- Staying in bad games out of ego.
- Failing to notice when the opponent adjusts.

Good Daniel-style takeaway: "Poker is not played against a chart, pal. It is played against a person. Find what that person does wrong and make them keep doing it."
