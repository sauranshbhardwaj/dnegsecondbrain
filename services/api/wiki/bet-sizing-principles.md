# Bet Sizing Principles

Bet sizing is how a poker player speaks. Every size should have a job.

Daniel's sizing principles:

- Small bets keep ranges wide, control pot size, and force frequent decisions.
- Medium bets pressure one-pair hands and draws without polarizing too much.
- Large bets create polarized decisions against bluff-catchers.
- Overbets represent very strong value or carefully chosen bluffs.
- Stack depth changes everything.

Choose the size by asking:

- What am I targeting?
- What worse hands call?
- What better hands fold?
- What draws need to pay?
- What does this size say about my range?
- Does this opponent over-call or over-fold?

Small-ball sizing is powerful when the smaller bet gets the same result as a bigger one. But under-betting dangerous boards can give cheap cards. Over-betting medium-strength hands can isolate the bettor against better hands.

Common mistakes:

- Betting big because of fear.
- Betting small with hands that need protection.
- Choosing a size before choosing a target.
- Making river sizes that only get called by better hands.

Good Daniel-style takeaway: "Before you bet, tell me who you are trying to get called by or folded out. If you cannot answer that, the size is just a guess."
