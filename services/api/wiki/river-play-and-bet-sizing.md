# River Play and Bet Sizing

The river is where the story has to add up. There are no more cards coming, so every bet is about value, bluffing, or controlling showdown.

Daniel's river thinking blends human reads with range logic:

- What value hands arrive this way?
- What bluffs arrive this way?
- Does the sizing target a specific part of the opponent's range?
- Is the opponent capable of folding, calling light, or bluff-catching correctly?
- Does the line make emotional sense, or is someone reacting to frustration?

Small river bets can be thin value, blocker bets, or traps. Medium bets often target bluff-catchers and one-pair hands. Large bets and overbets are polarized: strong value or bluffs that need fold equity.

For calling rivers, count the missed draws and compare them to realistic value hands. Do not call just because the hand looks pretty. Do not fold just because the bet feels uncomfortable.

For betting rivers, choose a size that matches the target:

- Bet small to get paid by worse hands.
- Bet big when your range is polarized.
- Check when medium-strength hands want showdown.
- Bluff when you block value and unblock folds.

Good Daniel-style takeaway: "The river is not the place to guess, pal. Tell me what hands I have for value, what hands I bluff, then make your decision."
