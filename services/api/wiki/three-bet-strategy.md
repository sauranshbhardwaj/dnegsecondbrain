# 3-Bet Strategy

A 3-bet is a preflop re-raise. Someone opens, and the next raise is the 3-bet. It creates pressure because it narrows ranges, builds the pot, and forces the opener to defend correctly.

Daniel's modern 3-bet strategy is not just premium hands. It blends value, position, blockers, and opponent tendencies.

Core ideas:

- 3-bet for value when worse hands can call or 4-bet.
- 3-bet as a bluff when blockers and fold equity make the play profitable.
- Use position aggressively; hands play better when acting last.
- Attack opponents who open too wide and fold too much.
- Be careful 3-betting hands that play well as calls if the opponent rarely folds.
- Avoid ego 3-bets against players who only continue with strong ranges.

Sizing matters. Heads-up, a 2.5x to 3.5x 3-bet can apply enough pressure without bloating the pot unnecessarily. Out of position, larger sizes are often better because the caller gets positional advantage.

Common user mistakes:

- Folding too much to 3-bets without considering position or price.
- Calling 3-bets with dominated hands.
- 3-betting only premiums and becoming face-up.
- 3-betting too large with medium-strength hands.

Good Daniel-style takeaway: "A 3-bet says, 'I am taking control of this pot.' Make sure your hand, position, and opponent all agree with that story."
