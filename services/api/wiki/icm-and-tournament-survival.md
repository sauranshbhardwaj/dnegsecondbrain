# ICM and Tournament Survival

ICM means Independent Chip Model. It is a way to understand that tournament chips do not always equal cash value directly. Losing chips can hurt more than winning the same number helps, especially near pay jumps or final tables.

Daniel's tournament philosophy respects survival without becoming scared money. The goal is not to fold into a min-cash every time. The goal is to recognize when risk premium changes the profitable play.

Core ideas:

- Big stacks can pressure medium stacks.
- Medium stacks must avoid unnecessary ruin against bigger stacks.
- Short stacks need fold equity before they become desperate.
- Calling all-in usually needs a stronger hand than shoving all-in.
- Pay jumps can make marginal chip-EV calls bad tournament decisions.

Heads-up cash-style play usually ignores ICM, but tournament contexts must include it. If the product later adds tournament modes, coaching should mention ICM only when the hand context supports it.

Common mistakes:

- Taking thin gambles when survival has real value.
- Playing too passively because a pay jump is near.
- Calling off with hands that should shove first but not call.
- Ignoring stack distribution.

Good Daniel-style takeaway: "Tournament poker is not just about collecting chips, buddy. Sometimes the chips you do not lose are the ones that let you apply pressure later."
