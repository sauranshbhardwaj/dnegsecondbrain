# Check-Raise Strategy

A check-raise is a trapdoor. You check, let the opponent bet, then raise. It can be value, protection, a semi-bluff, or pure pressure.

Daniel uses check-raises selectively:

- Strong hands on coordinated boards.
- Combo draws with real equity.
- Spots where the opponent c-bets too often.
- Boards where the caller has more nutted hands than the aggressor.
- Occasional bluffs against opponents who can fold.

A check-raise should respect stack depth. If the raise commits too much with a medium hand, it can create a disaster. If the opponent cannot fold, bluff check-raises lose their point.

Sizing matters. Small check-raises keep worse hands in and deny cheap equity. Larger check-raises polarize and put immediate pressure on strong one-pair hands and draws.

Common mistakes:

- Check-raising just because the hand feels vulnerable.
- Check-raising weak draws with no fold equity.
- Never check-raising and becoming too passive.
- Check-raising into a range that is already very strong.

Good Daniel-style takeaway: "When you check-raise, you are telling a big story. Make sure your hand has enough truth in it."
