# Small Ball Poker Philosophy

Small ball poker is about winning information and pressure without risking your whole stack every time you enter a pot. The goal is not to play tiny forever. The goal is to keep the pot manageable while you gather reads, preserve options, and create spots where your opponent makes the bigger mistake.

In Daniel Negreanu's style, small ball has a few core ideas:

- Play more hands in position, especially when you can control the size of the pot.
- Use smaller opens, smaller continuation bets, and probing bets to keep weaker hands in while learning where you stand.
- Avoid building huge pots with one-pair hands unless the opponent's tendencies justify it.
- Make opponents uncomfortable by forcing frequent decisions, not by risking everything on one street.
- Preserve your stack because future hands are part of the edge.

Small ball does not mean passive poker. It means selective pressure. A small bet can be a value bet, a blocker bet, a range bet, or a setup for a bigger river decision. The key is that each bet should ask the opponent a question.

Heads-up, small ball becomes especially powerful because ranges are wider and people defend too emotionally. A player who calls too much can be value-bet thinly. A player who folds too much can be pressured repeatedly. A player who fights every pot can be trapped with stronger ranges.

The danger is taking the philosophy too far. Small ball is not an excuse to call every hand, avoid value, or refuse to punish obvious weakness. When board texture, stack depth, and opponent tendencies call for a larger bet, use it. Daniel's modern approach blends small-ball instincts with solver-aware sizing: smaller bets when range pressure is enough, larger bets when polarization matters.

In post-hand coaching, use small ball to explain:

- Why a smaller bet could have achieved the same fold equity.
- Why pot control mattered with a medium-strength hand.
- Why a large bet was good when the hand was polarized.
- Why repeated small mistakes compound over a session.
- Why stack preservation creates future opportunities.

Good Daniel-style takeaway: "You do not need to win the whole stack every time you think you are ahead, buddy. Win the decision first. The chips follow."
