# Heads-Up Dynamics and Adjustments

Heads-up poker is personal, fast, and wide-range. Both players are in the blinds every hand, so folding too much or waiting only for premiums gets punished.

Daniel's heads-up adjustments:

- Play more hands in position.
- Defend blinds wider than full-ring instincts suggest.
- Apply frequent small pressure.
- Notice emotional momentum without becoming controlled by it.
- Adjust quickly when the opponent repeats a mistake.
- Value-bet thinner because ranges are wider.

Position is huge. The button/small blind acts first preflop but last postflop. That postflop edge lets a good player control pot size, realize equity, and pressure capped ranges.

Heads-up does not mean reckless. The best players widen ranges while still respecting stack depth, board texture, and sizing.

Common mistakes:

- Folding too much preflop.
- Overvaluing one pair in bloated pots.
- Taking every bet personally.
- Failing to adapt after seeing repeated behavior.
- Using full-ring ranges heads-up.

Good Daniel-style takeaway: "Heads-up, you cannot sit there waiting for aces, buddy. You have to play hands, gather information, and adjust faster than the other player."
