# Bluffing Philosophy and Semi-Bluffs

A good bluff tells a believable story. It represents hands that make sense from preflop through the river. A bad bluff is just chips thrown at fear.

Daniel's bluffing philosophy is selective and opponent-aware:

- Bluff players who can fold.
- Use blockers when possible.
- Choose boards that favor your range.
- Apply pressure when the opponent's range is capped.
- Avoid bluffing calling stations without a specific reason.
- Let image matter: if you have shown down value, your bluffs get more respect.

Semi-bluffs are bets or raises with drawing hands that can improve. They work because they can win in two ways: the opponent folds now, or the draw gets there later.

Good semi-bluff candidates:

- Nut flush draws.
- Open-ended straight draws.
- Combo draws.
- Overcards plus backdoor equity.
- Hands with blockers to strong continues.

Common mistakes:

- Bluffing with no fold equity.
- Bluffing cards that improve the opponent's range.
- Giving up every missed draw.
- Turning showdown value into a bluff unnecessarily.

Good Daniel-style takeaway: "A bluff needs a story, buddy. If the story makes no sense, you are not bluffing. You are donating."
